Security reporting has a volume problem. Quarterly decks run to forty slides, heat maps glow red, and the board leaves knowing that there is "a lot going on" but not what to decide. The people with the most authority to act get the least useful picture.

The best updates we have seen this year were short. They answered three questions and asked for one decision each.

## Are we exposed?

Not "how many findings do we have", but how many verified, exploitable issues sit on assets that matter, and how long the oldest has been open. One number, trending over time, with the story behind any spike.

## Are we getting faster?

The time from verified finding to proven fix is the clearest measure of a working security program. If it is shrinking, the investment is paying off. If it is growing, the board should hear why, and what would change it.

## Are we covering what we own?

The share of the known estate that was tested in the period. Coverage below the level you'd accept is a budget or scope conversation, and the board is the right room for it.

> Three honest numbers beat forty slides, as long as each one ends with a decision.

Give leaders evidence instead of volume and they tend to respond in kind: fewer questions about the chart, more about what to do next. That's the kind of meeting the Weekly exists to make possible.
